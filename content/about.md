I'm Goutham. At Akamai I build an opinionated Kubernetes platform as a product
— upstream Kubernetes and Cluster API underneath, a set of deliberate choices
on top.

The opinions are the product. Kubernetes will let you do very nearly anything,
which is not the same thing as helping you. A platform has to decide on its
users' behalf: what to expose, what to hide, which defaults to enforce, and
which escape hatches to leave unlocked. Hold too few opinions and you've
shipped raw Kubernetes with a support contract. Hold too many and you've built
a cage with good docs. Finding the balance between those two is the job.

Mr. Miyagi had it right, as usual: **"Balance good, karate good. Everything good.
Balance bad, better pack up, go home."** He was totally talking about platform
APIs. In practice, balance looks like control-plane networking,
certificate rotation, RBAC, container runtime configuration, and the long tail
of things that only break at scale, in clusters you don't own — fun innit?

I write here for people who work on this stuff — platform engineers, cluster
operators, anyone who has read a Kubernetes changelog on purpose. No schedule.
[til]({{< relref "/til" >}}) is the shorter version — a few sentences on
something small I worked out. Those are half for me, but they're public because
somebody else has almost certainly hit the same thing.
